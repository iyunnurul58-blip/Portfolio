Product Management Project

Click & Collect Feature Enhancement

Dibimbing.id • May 2026 – Present

Problem Statement

Click & Collect is unable to provide a fast-shopping experience on par with in-store shopping because the stock confirmation process is still manual (HQ-WA-store), causing a wait time of 2–4 hours, resulting in 65% of users dropping off during the location and stock selection stage.

Goals

  • Eliminate manual store confirmation by implementing an automated, real-time inventory engine.
  • Slash average order confirmation time from 2–4 hours to instant minutes.
  • Reduce the Click & Collect drop-off rate from 65% and increase overall completion rate by +25%.
  • Increase Click & Collect usage share by +45% and boost Month-1 retention from 20%.

Processes & Execution

Business & User Research: Analyzed quantitative data from a 45-user study (Gen Z, Millennial, Gen X) and store reviews to pinpoint friction points.

Root Cause & Ideation: Used Issue Trees and "How Might We" frameworks. Formulated core solutions: Real-Time Inventory Sync, Instant Confirmation Engine, and Smart Store Routing.

Requirements & UX Design: Drafted BRD, FSD, and PRD documentation. Visualized "Before vs. After" BPMN process flows and mapped screen navigation.

Prototyping & Usability Testing: Built interactive Figma/Visily prototypes comparing Control vs. Experiment variants. Tested with 5 participants evaluating Task Completion, Misclicks, Confusion Signals, and SEQ scores.

Feature Prioritization: Evaluated features using RICE scoring and an Effort vs. Impact matrix to define MVP scopes.

Outcomes & Strategic Reflection

  • Proven Usability Gains: Experiment variant reduced average misclicks and improved task ease.
  • Validated Impact: Real-time stock badges on product pages and multi-stage order tracking (Received → Preparing → Ready for Pickup) eliminated user anxiety.
  • Next Steps: Approved moving forward with full technical rollout (Phase-based roadmap with stage-by-stage ETA timers).

Business Analysis Project

Asset Quality Review

The University of Adelaide • Aug 2024 – Nov 2024

Problem Statement

AQR Asset Management needed to understand how US-listed stocks and ETFs behaved across two market regimes — the COVID-19 shock vs. the stable Non-COVID window — to inform portfolio selection, risk management, and factor exposure decisions.

Goals

  • Compare Stocks and ETFs performance across COVID and Non-COVID periods.
  • Identify key drivers of daily returns and the probability of an up-day.
  • Build models to predict direction of price movements and group securities by risk-return profiles.
  • Deliver actionable recommendations to the investment team.

Process & Analytical Tasks

CRSP-style daily panel data + Fama-French factors cleaned from 1.9M to 121.4K observations (17.9K ETF, 103.5K stock) with 22 engineered variables.

1. Descriptive Analysis: Distributions, central tendency, time-series of mean returns.

2. OLS Regression (5 specifications): Key drivers of daily return.

3. Logistic Regression: Probability of a positive daily price move.

4. K-Means Clustering: Unsupervised discovery of market regimes.

5. PCA: Dimensionality reduction explaining S&P 500 returns.

Outcomes & Key Findings

  • ETFs were markedly more stable than stocks — tighter spreads, lower volatility, smaller COVID drawdowns.
  • OLS models generalized best on ETFs (RMSE ≈ 0.06–0.12).
  • Liquidity & activity drive up-days during COVID: Volatility (+1.13), NUMTRD_Log (+1.49), BidAskSpread_Bps (+1.23), VOL_Log (+0.96).
  • K-Means uncovered 3 regimes: Calm, Stressed, and Active.
  • PCA: PC13 is the single strongest predictor of S&P return (coef ≈ 19.23), dominated by Risk-Free Rate (0.68) and Volatility (0.41).
