Business Analysis Project

Asset Quality Review

The University of Adelaide • Aug 2024 – Nov 2024

Problem Statement

AQR Asset Management needed to understand how US-listed stocks and ETFs behaved across two market regimes — the COVID-19 shock vs. the stable Non-COVID window — to inform portfolio selection, risk management, and factor exposure decisions.

Goals

  • Compare Stocks and ETFs performance across COVID and Non-COVID periods.
  • Identify key drivers of daily returns and the probability of an up-day.
  • Build models to predict direction of price movements and group securities by risk-return profiles.
  • Deliver actionable recommendations to the investment team.

Process & Execution

CRSP-style daily panel data + Fama-French factors cleaned from 1.9M to 121.4K observations (17.9K ETF, 103.5K stock) with 22 engineered variables.

1. Descriptive Analysis: Distributions, central tendency, time-series of mean returns.

2. OLS Regression (5 specifications): Key drivers of daily return.

3. Logistic Regression: Probability of a positive daily price move.

4. K-Means Clustering: Unsupervised discovery of market regimes.

5. PCA: Dimensionality reduction explaining S&P 500 returns.

Outcome & Key Findings

  • ETFs were markedly more stable than stocks — tighter spreads, lower volatility, smaller COVID drawdowns.
  • OLS models generalized best on ETFs (RMSE ≈ 0.06–0.12).
  • Liquidity & activity drive up-days during COVID: Volatility (+1.13), NUMTRD_Log (+1.49), BidAskSpread_Bps (+1.23), VOL_Log (+0.96).
  • K-Means uncovered 3 regimes: Calm, Stressed, and Active.
  • PCA: PC13 is the single strongest predictor of S&P return (coef ≈ 19.23), dominated by Risk-Free Rate (0.68) and Volatility (0.41).

Customer Analytics Project

Google Merchandise Store Performance Analysis

The University of Adelaide • May 2024 – Jun 2024

Problem Statement

Divergence Between Traffic Growth and Revenue Performance: The Google Merchandise Store experienced a drop in overall revenue and Average Order Value (AOV) despite a surge in user traffic and total transactions. This performance gap was driven by an unoptimized product mix, declining engagement among younger demographics (ages 18–24), and inefficient digital ad spend across high-cost marketing campaigns.

Goals

  • Identify Root Causes: Analyze transactional and behavioral data via Google Looker Studio to pinpoint drivers behind AOV and conversion rate drop-offs.
  • Optimize Product Mix: Identify high-margin merchandise to boost Average Order Value through targeted cross-selling strategies.
  • Improve Ad Spend Efficiency: Evaluate acquisition channels and Google Ads campaigns using ROAS and Cost per Transaction (CPT) metrics.

Process & Execution

  • Website & Behavioral Analytics: Evaluated engagement metrics (Bounce Rate, Session Duration) and demographic segments across age and gender.
  • Product Basket & Margin Audit: Analyzed sales volume, revenue contribution, and unit margins across 5 core product categories (Notebooks, Caps, T-shirts, Bags, Mugs).
  • Channel & Campaign Evaluation: Mapped conversion performance across acquisition channels (Direct, Social, Organic) and audited ad efficiency across 4 seasonal Google Ads campaigns.

Outcome & Key Findings

  • Channel Strategy: Direct emerged as the highest-converting channel (9.68% CR, $206K revenue, 1,969 transactions), whereas Social was the primary driver for top-of-funnel traffic (22.2K users).
  • High-Margin Product Discovery: Caps generated the highest revenue per unit ($160.41/unit) and proved most popular among female buyers ($31.81K). Expanding cap variants was recommended to re-engage younger audiences (ages 18–24).
  • Seasonal Campaign Optimization: The Summer Campaign proved to be the most cost-effective (lowest CPT at $36.91), while the Winter Campaign yielded the highest return on ad investment (ROAS $2.86). Reallocating budget away from underperforming campaigns like Fall Promo (CPT $45.74) was advised.
